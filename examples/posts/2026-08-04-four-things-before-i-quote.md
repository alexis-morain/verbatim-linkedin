Four things, and I will not put a number on a mandate before I have all four.

This is not a qualification ritual. Each one of them has, at least once,
changed the price I gave or made me say no, and I can date each of those.

The last three months of the close, as they actually shipped. Not the
policy, the files. What I am reading is how late they were and how much moved
after they were sent. A close that lands on the fourth working day and never
restates tells me the ledger is somebody else's problem. A close that restates
twice a quarter tells me my first two months are not forecasting at all.

Who signs. One name, or a founder and an investor who disagree. The second
version is a different job and it is the one I priced wrong for a year. A model
that has to survive two readers with different objectives is not a bigger
model, it is a different document, and the argument in the room is the work.

The next money event and its date. A raise in October and a raise
some day are not the same engagement. If there is a date, the whole mandate
bends toward the data room.

Whether anything in the last twelve months is being restated. This is the one
people do not volunteer. A restatement is not a red flag, it is a workload with
a deadline set by somebody outside the company.

Nothing on that list is financial analysis. All four are answerable in a
twenty minute call, by a founder, with no preparation.

If you are about to hire a fractional CFO, anybody's, take that list into the
first conversation and make them answer it back to you. The ones who cannot
will quote you a day rate and find the work later, which is what I did for three
mandates.

Nadia Feriel, fractional CFO for seed and Series A B2B SaaS.
I build the model and I sit in the room when it gets argued about.

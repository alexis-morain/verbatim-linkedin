## Profile

### What I sell

Fractional CFO work for seed and Series A B2B SaaS, three to five days a month.
I build the model, run the board pack, and sit in the room when the numbers are
argued about.

What separates me from a bookkeeper or an outsourced accounting firm: I am
accountable for the forecast, not for the ledger. Somebody else closes the
month. I am the one who says whether the plan survives it.

### Who I write to

**Priority 1.** Founders of seed to Series A B2B SaaS, 5 to 40 people, usually
technical, usually without a finance hire. They read here, they buy a fractional
day rate, and they are one bad board meeting away from wanting help.

**Priority 2.** Seed fund partners and platform leads who get asked for a CFO
referral three times a quarter. Slower, but one relationship produces several
mandates.

**Out of scope on this channel.** Series C and later. They hire a full time VP
Finance, and the decision runs through a search firm, not through a feed.

**Abandoned.** Agency and services businesses, tested from January to April
2026. Eleven conversations, two proposals, zero signed. They wanted bookkeeping
at a CFO price and said so politely. Not a targeting failure, a product
mismatch. Do not write posts that speak to them.

### What this channel is for

1. Being referred by investors. One warm introduction from a fund is worth more
   than a month of anything else here, and it is the mechanism that produced
   three of my last five mandates.
2. Direct inbound from founders. Slower, less predictable, better margins.

It is not a prospecting channel. Nothing here is cold outreach.

### Objective and cadence

- Posts per period: 6 per month, counted monthly and not weekly
- Business objective: trust
- Decision threshold: if 18 posts produce zero inbound message that mentions a
  mandate, the channel is not doing its job and I re-open the question.

### Core conviction

A startup's financial model is a communication artifact before it is a
forecasting one. Founders think they are getting the numbers wrong. Most of
them are getting the argument wrong, and the numbers are fine.

### What I fight

The board deck that reports last quarter and forecasts nothing. It is the
default output of every founder who has been told to "be data driven", and it
converts a room full of expensive people into an audience.

### What I can prove

- Runway model rebuilt for a 22 person Series A: forecast error on net burn
  went from 31 percent to 6 percent over two quarters. Numbers from their own
  monthly close, used with permission, published on my site.
- Median time I spend on a first board pack: 11 hours. Measured across 9
  engagements in my own time tracking.
- A seed round where the diligence question that stalled it for five weeks was
  a revenue recognition policy nobody had written down. Public, the founder has
  spoken about it on a podcast.
- My own rate card, which is on my site.

### True, but needs a call before it is published

Client burn figures, valuations, anything from a live fundraise, and the two
mandates that ended badly.

### Names I must never cite

Aperture Labs, Kettleford, and the fund that referred both. Everything on the
case studies page is already public and free to use.

### What I never say

- A number I have not measured.
- A client experience I have not lived.
- A promise about a fundraise outcome, which I do not control.
- Anything that implies I am providing regulated financial advice.

### Hard rules

From `locales/en/style.md`, which is authoritative:

- Open with the answer, never with context.
- One idea per paragraph, three or four sentences.
- Take a position.
- End on the last useful idea. No summary.
- No em dash, no emoji.

### Traits observed in the corpus

Each one is followed by the sentence it was read from. A trait without a quote
is a guess and does not belong here.

**Opens on a number that sounds wrong.** Not on a claim, on a figure the reader
has to reconcile.

> "Eleven hours. That is the median time I spend building a first board pack,
> and most of it is not spent on the numbers."

**Names the reader's likely objection and answers it inside the same
paragraph**, rather than saving it for the end.

> "You will say the model was wrong. It was not, particularly. The commentary
> around it was."

**Second person, no contractions in the argument, contractions in the asides.**
The register tightens when she is making the point and loosens when she is
telling you about a room.

> "You do not need a better forecast. You need a shorter one." / "Anyway, the
> partner had stopped reading by slide four."

**Ends on a decision, not a lesson.** No moral, no call to reflect.

> "We deleted eleven slides and kept the cash chart. The next meeting ran
> forty minutes."

**Length between 900 and 1,600 characters** across the three posts. Shorter
than the platform average and it holds, because there is one idea per post.

### Pillar 2. What I actually do in the room. 3 of 6

**Effect on the reader: trust.**

The mechanics. How a first board pack gets built, what gets deleted, which
policy has to be written down before a data room opens.

**Verifiability requirement:** a post in this pillar carries at least one
measured element, a number or a dated decision. Without one it belongs in
pillar 1, or it does not publish. This pillar dies quietly if it is allowed to
run on opinion.

### Pillar 3. Decisions in public. 1 of 6

**Effect on the reader: connection.**

Mandates that ended badly, the segment I abandoned, the things I priced wrong.
Dated, specific, no moral at the end.

It only works while it stays factual. A failure told without a number is a
posture, not a post.

# 2026-07-02, The eleven slides we deleted

Eleven slides. That is what we deleted from a board deck last month, and the
meeting that followed ran forty minutes instead of two hours.

You will say the model was wrong. It was not, particularly. The numbers had
been closed by a competent accountant and they tied out. What was wrong was
that the deck answered a question nobody in the room had asked: what happened.

Everybody already knew what happened. They had read the update. What they came
for was the decision, and the decision was four slides in, after a revenue
chart by region that exists because a template had a slot for it.

So we cut. Anything reporting a number that had already been sent by email went
out. Anything that could not change what somebody in the room would do next
went out. What was left was a cash chart, a hiring plan, and one question with
two options and a recommendation.

The partner who usually stops reading by slide four asked about the second
option. That is the whole point. A board pack is not a report you present to a
room, it is an argument you hand to people who are going to disagree with parts
of it.

We deleted eleven slides and kept the cash chart. The next meeting ran forty
minutes.

# Market conventions: English

Jurisdictions: United States and United Kingdom, stated separately because the
obligations differ.
Last reviewed: 2026-08-27.

Not legal advice. Check the regulator, and re-date this file if it is more than
a year old.

## Disclosure obligations

**United States.** The FTC Endorsement Guides (16 CFR Part 255) require a clear
and conspicuous disclosure of any material connection between an endorser and
a brand: payment, free product, affiliate commission, employment, a family
relationship. "Clear and conspicuous" means in the post itself, hard to miss,
not in a comment and not behind a "more" link.

**United Kingdom.** The CAP Code requires advertising to be obviously
identifiable as such. The ASA and the CMA have both acted on undisclosed paid
posts. The accepted markers are `#ad` or the word "Advert" placed up front, not
at the end.

Wording that satisfies both, and costs nothing:

> Affiliate link, I earn a commission on it.

Placed in the post body, above the fold if the link is above the fold.

**The documented trap.** In this project's own history, an affiliate post
carried the disclosure in the draft and lost it in the published version. A
disclosure is not a style detail. It does not get cut when the post gets
shortened.

## Platform conventions in this market

**First names, contractions, and short paragraphs** are the norm on English
LinkedIn across B2B. Formal register reads as corporate communications, which
is a position rather than a politeness.

**Numbers are stated less freely than a European reader expects** in some
segments, particularly where the audience includes employers or public
companies. This is a market fact, not a style rule: decide it in the profile.

## What reads as imported

Habits that mark an English post as translated:

- A space before `: ; ! ?`, which is French typography.
- Guillemets `« »` instead of quotation marks.
- Opening on a subject announcement rather than on the claim.
- Formal address constructions rendered literally, producing sentences nobody
  says out loud.

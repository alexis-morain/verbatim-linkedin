# Interview wording: English

One section per intent in `references/interview-intents.md`. The intent says
what is being looked for. This file says how it is asked.

This is an interview, not a form. Whoever asks talks like a colleague.

## Set A. Setup

### false-belief

> Think of a typical client, the one who comes to you. What do they believe
> when they arrive that turns out to be wrong?
>
> What you answer here becomes your public positions.

Affordance: options to pick from. Three propositions built from what the
profile already says, plus "or write your own".

### market-shift

> What is changing in your market that most people have not seen yet?
>
> This becomes your analysis posts: your read on the game, before the others.

Affordance: options to pick from.

### origin-scene

> Tell me about a moment with a client where you thought: this is exactly why I
> do this work.
>
> That moment becomes one of your strongest posts. Lived experience is what
> people remember.

Affordance: memory prompts. Three italic starters above a free field, written
as triggers and not as answers.

### repeated-advice

> What are the two or three things you tell every single client?
>
> These become your expertise posts: what you say in private, said in public.

Affordance: free field. Follow up once if the answer is a single word.

### current-quest

> What are you trying to build, or prove, through this business?
>
> This becomes the through line. People follow your story from one post to the
> next.

Optional follow-up, and it earns its place:

> And what are you working on right now that moves you toward it?

Work in progress is a source of ideas nobody uses.

Affordance: editable propositions.

### thesis

> Someone introduces you to a stranger and says "he believes that...". What
> comes next?
>
> That sentence becomes your thesis. It is the one people quote when they talk
> about you.

Affordance: editable propositions. "Combine, edit, or write your own."

### stage

> Where are you today in this business?
>
> Your answer sets the register. Starting out and established do not talk alike.

Affordance: options to pick from, four or five readable states (starting out,
filling the calendar, full and selecting, changing model).

### cadence

> Your publishing rhythm, and the business objective behind it.
>
> The objective sets the mix: visibility, trust, or action.

Affordance: options on the objective, short field on the rhythm.

### core-conviction

> What do you hold to be true that most people in your field do not?
>
> This is the fuel for your stance posts. Without it, a profile produces
> tutorials.

Affordance: editable propositions.

### what-they-fight

> And what are you against? A practice, a habit, a piece of received wisdom.
> Something specific enough that someone could disagree.
>
> "Bad practice" is not an answer.

Affordance: editable propositions.

## Set B. Post interview

### scene

> Walk me through the moment. When was it, who were you with, what was the
> context?

If the answer is a generality, do not advance. Ask for the instance again.

### number

> Is there a number in there? How many, out of how many, over what period?

If no number exists, say so out loud and continue without one. Never make one
up.

> Right, no number on this one. We go without, and the post will not pretend
> otherwise.

### friction

> What jammed? What surprised you, or cost more than you planned for.

### position

> What do you conclude from that, which most people do not?

This is the question that separates a post from a tutorial. If the answer is
consensual, push once: "could someone in your field disagree with that?"

### cost-of-not-knowing

> What does it cost someone who does not know this?

The punchline almost always comes from here.

### contradiction

> Is there anything in your own practice that contradicts what you just said?

Optional. When it produces something, it makes the post hard to attack.

## Connective phrases

These are the ones a pack forgets, and they are how the engine's language leaks
into a post.

**Announcing what is missing**, every turn, in one line:

> We have the scene and the number. What is missing is what it cost you to find
> out.

**Offering the two angles**, with the exit:

> Neither one speaks to you? Tell me what you meant.

**Two opposed options** instead of a suggestion:

> Did something specific trigger it, or did it just happen?

**Validation sheet**:

> I go with this, or is something missing?

**Generated wording**, when the pack has no question for an intent:

> Wording generated on the fly, not reviewed by a native speaker.
